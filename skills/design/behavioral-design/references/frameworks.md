## Evidence grading

| Lever | Grade | Basis and caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defaults (opt-out over opt-in) | Strong | Pension auto-enrolment (Madrian & Shea, 2001) raised new-hire participation to 86 %; organ-donor defaults (Johnson & Goldstein, 2003). Effects are large and persistent when opting out stays easy |
| Removing steps and friction | Strong | Consistent across field trials; the size depends on how much friction existed |
| Implementation intentions ("when X, I will Y") | Moderate | Meta-analysis reports a medium effect on goal attainment; weaker for complex or long-horizon goals |
| Timely prompts and reminders | Moderate | Reliable small effects; decay when repeated without change |
| Descriptive social norms | Moderate | Works when the norm is true and favourable; can backfire when it reveals that most people do not comply |
| Fresh-start timing | Moderate | Field evidence for goal initiation at temporal landmarks; small absolute effects |
| Modelling by leaders and champions | Moderate | Consistent in organisational change studies; depends on the model being credible to the actors |
| Worked examples and training | Moderate | Strong when the barrier is `psychological-capability`; near zero against any other barrier |
| Incentives and removing conflicting incentives | Mixed | Removing a conflicting incentive is reliable; adding a new reward can crowd out intrinsic motivation and stop working when it ends |
| Information and education alone | Weak | Rarely changes behavior unless the barrier is genuinely `psychological-capability` |
| Nudges in general | Mixed | Academic trials averaged about 8.7 percentage points but at-scale nudge-unit trials about 1.4 points (DellaVigna & Linos, *Econometrica*, 2022); a large meta-analysis (Mertens et al., *PNAS*, 2022) reported a medium effect that shrinks toward zero after correcting for publication bias (Maier et al., *PNAS*, 2022). Present nudges as cheap, small, and worth testing, never as guaranteed |

## What not to cite as settled

- **Behavioral priming** (e.g. reading age-related words makes people walk slower) — central findings failed to replicate; Kahneman publicly acknowledged over-relying on them in *Thinking, Fast and Slow*.
- **Ego depletion** (willpower as a depletable resource) — a multi-lab registered replication (Hagger et al., 2016) found an effect near zero.
- **Any single headline effect size** without the at-scale comparison above.

Use these as cautionary examples in a workshop if useful; they make the point that a plausible mechanism is not evidence.

## Further reading

- Michie, Atkins & West, *The Behaviour Change Wheel: A Guide to Designing Interventions* (2014) — the full intervention-function and policy-category mapping.
- Katy Milkman, *How to Change* (2021) — field-tested levers for individuals and teams.
- Everett Rogers, *Diffusion of Innovations* — adopter categories, useful when a rollout stalls after the early adopters.
